## Scene composition

Across the **horizontal centre** of the frame, **four cluster-icons**
arranged in a row from left to right, equally spaced. Each cluster is
**a small group of 3-4 abstract figure-icons** standing close together
(back-view, no faces, electric-blue and teal alternating silhouettes,
simple flat fills).

- **Cluster 1** (far left): an originating body — slightly smaller,
  rendered in cooler tones (more electric-blue, lower intensity).
- **Cluster 2** (centre-left): the focal body — rendered with **a
  warm amber accent halo** surrounding the cluster, signalling that
  this is the step the slide is about. Figures in the cluster are at
  full intensity.
- **Cluster 3** (centre-right): an intermediate body — neutral, similar
  to cluster 1.
- **Cluster 4** (far right): the receiving body — rendered slightly
  brighter than 1 and 3 to suggest "destination", but without the amber
  halo of cluster 2.

**Between each pair of clusters**, a **single thin arrow** (teal with
soft glow) points from the left cluster to the right cluster,
indicating the flow of the dossier through the procession. The arrows
are simple, clean, geometric — no decorative flourishes.

Above the row of clusters, in the upper portion of the frame, a faint
constellation of teal nodes (very sparse, low intensity) suggests the
broader institutional context within which the procession unfolds.

Generous dark navy negative space below the clusters and around the
arrows.

## Palette usage

- `bg-navy` — dominant canvas
- `electric-blue` — half the figures in each cluster + faint upper
  constellation
- `teal` — half the figures + the arrows between clusters
- `amber` — the halo around the focal cluster (the single luminous
  focal element of the composition)
- `coral` — not used (this is not a political-dimension scene)
- `white` — not used

---

## When to use

Slides explaining an institutional decision path: the dossier travels
through Body A → Body B → Body C → Body D, and the slide is specifically
about the role of one body in that chain. The amber halo highlights
which body the slide is about.

Particularly fits "the institutional decision path" slide of a
governance proposal, where the message is "here is where this slide's
audience sits in the procession".

---

## Variation rules

**Must stay structurally identical**:

- A linear horizontal row (not a cycle, not a tree, not a hub-and-spoke).
- Four cluster-icons (or 3 minimum, 5 maximum) in sequence.
- One arrow between each pair of clusters, all pointing in the same
  direction.
- Exactly one cluster carries the amber halo (the focal step).
- Faint constellation above (institutional context).

**Can be adapted**:

- The number of clusters (3 to 5 — fewer becomes a single transition,
  more becomes crowded).
- Which cluster carries the amber halo — depends on the slide's
  audience / focal step.
- The composition of each cluster (number of figures per cluster, 2 to 5).
- Direction of flow (left → right by default; can mirror right → left if
  the procession reads better that way for the audience).

**Forbidden variations**:

- Branching paths (use a different archetype for decision trees).
- Two clusters with amber halos (breaks the single-focal-element rule).
- Bidirectional arrows / loops (defeats the procession reading; if the
  dossier loops back, use `cycle.md` instead).
- Numbered or labelled clusters (no text rule).
- Realistic conference rooms or buildings around each cluster (too
  literal; the abstract clusters carry the meaning).
