## Scene composition

Two stylised academic building icons stand on opposite cliff edges in the
middle of the frame, separated by a deep dark abyss in the lower-centre.

- **Left building**: classical pediment with two columns, electric-blue
  fill with subtle inner gradient, slightly glowing.
- **Right building**: simple rectangle with soft rounded corners and a few
  small lit windows, teal fill with subtle inner gradient.

Across the abyss, **a luminous amber glowing arc** spans from the left
building to the right building — a single bridge of warm light, with a
soft amber halo extending into the dark space.

Above the bridge, in the upper portion of the frame, a small constellation
of teal nodes connected by thin teal lines arcs across the dark sky, with
**one amber accent node at the centre** — ambient digital intelligence
presiding over the transition.

Generous dark navy negative space dominates the lower abyss and the
surrounding sky.

## Palette usage

- `bg-navy` — dominant canvas (sky + abyss)
- `electric-blue` — left (classical) building icon
- `teal` — right (modern) building icon + constellation lines and nodes
- `amber` — the bridge arc (the focal warm element) + the central
  constellation node
- `white` — not used here (or only as a faint pin-point highlight on the
  amber bridge if needed)

## When to use

Title slides, opening slides, closing slides, transition-themed slides
where the message is "we cross from past to future / from one institution
to another / from one era to another via a single luminous link".

---

## Variation rules

**Must stay structurally identical**:

- Two building icons separated by an abyss.
- The amber bridge as the single luminous focal element.
- Generous dark negative space in the abyss and sky.
- One bridge, never two, never a network of crossings.

**Can be adapted**:

- The specific shapes of the building icons (pediment + columns is the
  default for "academic"; substitute simple geometric icons for "research
  centre", "company", "government", etc.).
- The constellation above can be denser (closing slide) or sparser (title
  slide) depending on the narrative beat.
- The distance between the buildings (wider abyss = more dramatic
  transition).

**Forbidden variations**:

- Multiple bridges (breaks the single-focal-element rule).
- Bright daylight sky (the canvas must stay dark).
- Realistic buildings or photographic details.
- Adding a figure on the bridge (creates a second focal point).
