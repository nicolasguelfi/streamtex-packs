## Scene composition

In the **centre** of the frame, **a stylised balance scale** rendered as
flat geometric shapes — a single thin vertical post rising from a small
electric-blue base, with a horizontal beam balanced on top. The beam is
**level** (perfectly horizontal — neither side dipping).

At each end of the beam, **a small pan** rendered as a shallow
half-circle in soft teal-electric tone, suspended by two thin lines.

- **Left pan**: a small stack of three nested **abstract digital
  document icons** (electric-blue flat shapes — the documents are clearly
  digital tokens, never paper sheets, never scrolls; think simple
  rectangles with subtle inner gradient and a single small notch).
- **Right pan**: a small **architectural icon** of a multi-zone building
  (teal flat shape — a simple rectangle subdivided into 3-4 small zone
  cells, suggesting modular calibrated architecture).

Behind the scale, **a subtle ambient EU-stars hint** — twelve faint dots
in cyan-mist colour at very low opacity (around 15-20 %), arranged in a
faint circle, never literal, just an atmospheric trace.

The **scale beam glows softly** at the pivot point with a single small
amber accent — the focal warmth of the composition, signaling the
proportionality decision being weighed.

Generous dark navy negative space above and around the scale.

## Palette usage

- `bg-navy` — dominant canvas
- `electric-blue` — left pan (document icons) + base of the scale
- `teal` — right pan (architecture icon) + the pans themselves and the beam
- `amber` — single accent at the pivot point of the beam (focal warmth)
- `white` — not used here; very low-opacity cyan-mist for the EU-stars hint
- `coral` — not used here

---

## When to use

Slides about regulation, proportionality, evidence vs implementation,
trade-offs between two valid concerns. Particularly fits regulatory-policy
slides (GDPR, NIS2, AI Act), architecture-justification slides where the
message is "the architecture is calibrated to the regulatory requirement",
or any slide weighing two legitimate values.

The **EU-stars hint** is the only ambient cultural marker permitted in
GSE (per `graphic-line.md §9`); use it ONLY for slides explicitly about
European regulation. For non-regulatory balance scenes, omit the
EU-stars and let the scale stand alone.

## Variation rules

**Must stay structurally identical**:

- A single balance scale, level (never tipped).
- Two pans, each holding one small symbolic icon.
- Single amber accent at the beam pivot.
- Centred composition (the pivot of the beam is on the vertical axis).

**Can be adapted**:

- The icons in each pan — substitute for any two values being weighed
  (e.g., privacy icon ↔ utility icon; openness icon ↔ confidentiality icon).
  Keep them as simple flat single-shape icons in palette colours.
- Presence or absence of the EU-stars hint (regulatory vs general balance).
- The orientation can shift to square (1:1) by tightening the negative
  space — the composition itself stays centred.

**Forbidden variations**:

- Tipped scale (defeats the equilibrium message; if you want to show
  imbalance, use a different archetype).
- Heavy ornate scale (Lady Justice statue, blindfolded figure, etc.) —
  too literal; keep it abstract and geometric.
- Paper sheets or scrolls in the pans (paper-era objects, per §9
  forbidden list — use abstract digital document icons instead).
- More than two pans (defeats the binary-balance reading).
